# Subject line options

## Prompt

```text
Give me eight subject lines.

Message: WHAT IT IS ABOUT.
Reader: WHO THEY ARE and what they already know.
What I want them to do: ACTION.

Use eight different angles, not eight rewordings of one. Across the
set, cover at least: the direct statement, the specific fact, the
question, the deadline, the consequence of ignoring it, and the plain
label with no persuasion in it at all.

For each line give me three things on one row.

The line, with its character count.
The promise. What the reader now expects to find inside.
Delivers or overpromises. Given what I told you the message contains,
say whether the body can keep that promise. Overpromising is a specific
finding, not a style note, so name it when you see it.

Then one line: which option you would pick for this reader, and the
single reason why.

No emoji. No colon and dash construction on every line. No word that
would be read as marketing by someone who reads mostly internal mail.

MESSAGE SUMMARY:
```

## Before you run it

- Tell it what the body actually contains. Without that, the overpromise column is guesswork and the whole exercise turns into a thesaurus.
- The plain label option is there deliberately. For most internal mail it wins, and it is the one nobody generates.

## What you get

Eight genuinely different openings, each with the expectation it sets, and an explicit flag on the ones your message cannot live up to.

## Boundaries

It does not predict an open rate and it does not know your audience. The options are drafts, and the reason to pick one is your read of the reader rather than the model's.
