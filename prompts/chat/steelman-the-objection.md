# Steelman the objection

## Prompt

```text
Argue against my position. Make the objections as strong as an informed
opponent would make them.

My position: STATE IT.
My reasoning: THE ACTUAL REASONS, including the weak ones.
Who objects: WHO THEY ARE and what they are responsible for.

Give me three or four objections, strongest first.

For each one.

The objection, in the objector's own likely words, one paragraph.
What it rests on: a fact, a value, an incentive, or a past experience.
Naming this matters, because a values objection does not yield to
evidence.
The best answer available from what I told you. If my material does not
contain one, write NO ANSWER IN WHAT YOU GAVE ME.
What evidence would actually settle it, if any would.

Then one closing section: which objection has no good answer. Say it
plainly. If I have a real weakness, tell me now rather than letting the
room find it.

Do not be polite about this, and do not manufacture a rebuttal I cannot
support.
```

## Before you run it

- Include your weak reasons. A steelman built on your edited case attacks a position you are not actually holding.
- Say what the objector is responsible for. Most real objections come from someone's accountability rather than from the argument.

## What you get

The strongest version of the case against you, sorted by strength, with your answers separated from your gaps.

## Boundaries

It does not know your organisation, your history or what the room will actually say. The objections are a draft rehearsal, and the closing section is a prompt to go and check something rather than a verdict on your position.
