# Tradeoff frame

## Prompt

```text
Frame the trade-off in this decision.

Decision: WHAT.
The positions people are taking: SUMMARISE THEM.

Five parts.

The trade-off, in one sentence, stated as two good things that cannot
both be maximised. If your sentence has a villain in it, you have not
found the trade-off yet.

Who bears each cost. For each side, which group pays, in what currency:
time, money, risk, autonomy, credibility, workload. Disagreements
usually survive because the two sides are not paying with the same
thing.

When each side is right. The conditions under which choosing A is
correct, and the conditions under which choosing B is. Concrete
conditions, the kind you could check.

Which conditions hold here. Only from what I told you. Where I have not
told you, say what to check rather than filling it in.

Is it a false choice. Whether a third option gets most of both, and
what it would cost. Say plainly if there is not one, because an
invented third option is how a real trade-off gets buried.

Take no side.
```

## Before you run it

- Summarise both positions as their holders would state them. A trade-off framed from one side produces a frame that side already agrees with.
- The costs section is usually the unlock. Two groups paying in different currencies will argue indefinitely while both describe the same facts.

## What you get

The disagreement stated as a genuine tension rather than a misunderstanding, with the conditions that would settle it and an honest read on whether a third way exists.

## Boundaries

It does not decide, and it does not know the history behind the positions. The frame is a draft for the room to argue with, and the conditions it lists still need checking.
