# Second order effects

## Prompt

```text
Trace the effects of this decision past the one I intend.

Decision: WHAT CHANGES.
Intended effect: WHAT I WANT.
Who is affected: GROUPS, and how each is currently measured or paid.

Three layers.

First order. The direct effect, including the ones I did not mention.
Second order. What happens because of the first order effects,
especially how each affected group changes its behaviour once it
understands the new rule. Give the mechanism, not just the outcome: who
does what differently, and why it is rational for them.
Third order. What follows from that, where it is reasonably traceable.
Stop where it becomes speculation, and say where you stopped.

Then two sections that matter more than the layers.

How it gets gamed. If I am measuring something new, name the cheapest
way to satisfy the measure without doing the work. Somebody will find
it, so it may as well be now.
What to watch. Two or three observable signals that would tell me a
second order effect has started, each with where it would show up
first.

Be concrete about mechanisms. Skip anything that is just a restatement
of the decision.
```

## Before you run it

- Say how people are measured today. Most second order effects are a collision between the new rule and an old incentive, and without the old one the model can only guess.
- The gaming section is not cynicism. It is the fastest way to find out whether the measure measures the thing.

## What you get

A traced chain with the mechanism named at each step, the obvious gaming route written down, and a short list of signals to watch for.

## Boundaries

It does not know your people or your history, so treat every effect as a hypothesis with a mechanism attached. The trace is a draft, and the watch list is worth more than the predictions.
